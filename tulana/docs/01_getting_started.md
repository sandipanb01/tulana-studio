# Getting started

Tulana builds a **parallel corpus** from Indian school textbooks. You open the
English edition and its translation side by side, select the blocks that say the
same thing on each side, and save the pair. The corpus is then exported in
whichever format the next tool needs.

There are four tabs, and you will spend nearly all your time in the first.

**Blocks** — both editions side by side, with the blocks a document parser has
already found on each page. This is where the work happens.

**Saved pairs** — everything you have saved, to review, rename, approve or
correct.

**Export** — the corpus in eleven formats, from JSONL to translation memory.

**Guide** — these manuals.

## Your first pair, in about a minute

1. In **Blocks**, choose a board and class, then the target language, then the
   two editions. Press **Open side by side**.
2. Click a block on the English page. It highlights, and its text appears in the
   panel below.
3. Click the matching block on the target page.
4. Type a short name — *Practice set 1.1*, say — and press **Save pair**.

That is the whole loop. Everything else is refinement.

## Nothing is lost if you stop

Your selection is written to the server as you work, about a second and a half
after you stop clicking, and again as the tab closes. Close the browser
mid-selection and it is still there when you return. The panel says *Kept
automatically* when this has happened, and says so plainly if it could not.

Pressing **Save pair** turns that draft into an ordinary pair. Until then it
stays out of the corpus and out of exports.

## Who you are

Type your name in the box at the top right. It is recorded against everything
you save, so a reviewer can ask you about a pair later. It is stored on your own
device and never leaves it except attached to your work.
