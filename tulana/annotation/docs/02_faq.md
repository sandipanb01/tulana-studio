# Common questions

These are the questions a newcomer is most likely to have, grouped by topic. The manual explains each area at length. These answers are short and point you back to it. Where the honest answer is "we do not know yet" or "ask your supervisor", the answer says so.

## Getting started

**Do I need to install anything, and which browser should I use?**
You need nothing installed. Open the link you were given in a browser on a laptop or desktop computer. This guide was checked in Chrome. Firefox and Safari have not been checked, so if something looks wrong in one of them, switch to Chrome.

**The link will not open, or it opens a page that is not Setu. What now?**
The link is temporary. It stops working when whoever started Setu closes it, and this kind of link expires after about a week. If the page opens but looks like a different tool, add `/work/` to the end of the address. Otherwise ask whoever gave you the link for the current one. Your work is not affected, because it is kept on their computer.

**A box is empty, or the book I was asked to check is not in the list.**
The boxes list only books that have been added to this copy of Setu, and each box depends on the one above it, so work from the top down. A board that has only one language sits at the bottom of the first box under "only one language — cannot be paired", and it cannot be opened. If your book is not there, tell your supervisor the board, class, subject and language.

**What does "no scan on disk" mean? Is something wrong?**
Nothing is wrong. A scan is a picture of a printed page. The pictures are very large files, so they are often not on the computer that runs Setu, and the line under a book's title then ends with "· no scan on disk". In the Text view that is the only mention, and it says only that the picture is missing. The text you check is there. If you switch to the Page + blocks view without the pictures, a box says "No scan of this page on this machine — showing the text instead", and the text appears under it. Everything you need for checking translations works without scans.

## Losing work, and coming back

**Will I lose my work if I close the tab or my laptop dies?**
No. Answers, pairings, notes and corrections are saved on the computer that runs Setu, not on yours, as you make them. The one thing at risk is what you typed in the last second or two. Text typed into a passage is also kept in your browser until the computer that runs Setu confirms it. Before you close anything, look at the small message beside the counter at the top right. It should say "All changes saved" or "Nothing to save". After typing a note, wait a second or click outside the note box.

**Do I have to press Save?**
No. There is no save button. An answer is saved the moment you press it: the button turns solid teal and "Saved." appears beside the note box. A pairing is saved the moment you press **⇄ Pair these two**, and an unpairing the moment you confirm it. A note is saved about a second after you stop typing, and again when you click outside the note box. Text you correct in a passage is saved about a second after you stop typing, and straight away when you click outside its box. If a red message appears at the bottom of the screen after you press an answer, or pair or unpair, that did not go through. For typed text, watch the message at the top right, which the next question explains.

**What does the small message at the top right ("Saving…", "All changes saved") mean?**
It sits beside the counter once two books are open, and it reports on the text you type into passages. "Nothing to save" means you have not corrected anything since the page opened. "Saving…" means text is waiting to be sent, which usually lasts a second or two. "All changes saved" means everything you typed has reached the computer that runs Setu. "Not saved yet — trying again" (amber) means the text could not be sent, so Setu keeps it in your browser and tries again every few seconds, waiting longer each time up to half a minute. "Offline — kept on this computer" (amber) means your browser says you have no connection, and the text goes by itself when it comes back. "1 change needs you" (red) means Setu cannot save something without a decision from you, so look for a box asking which version to keep, or for a red message saying a change was refused. Answers, notes and pairings are not reported here. An answer shows "Saved." beside the note box, or a red message if it failed.

**What happens if I close the tab or the window while something is unsaved?**
If text you typed into a passage has not been confirmed yet, your browser asks whether you really want to leave. The words are your browser's own, and "Leave site?" is the usual question. Choose to stay, wait for the message at the top right to say "All changes saved", and then close the tab. If you leave anyway, Setu tries to send the text as the page closes and keeps a copy in your browser. The next time you open Setu in the same browser, a message says "Sending 1 change from last time that had not reached the server yet…" and the text goes through. That copy lives in one browser on one computer, so it is not there if you open Setu in another browser, on another computer, or in a private window. The question does not appear for notes, so wait a second after typing a note, or click outside the note box, before you close the tab.

**Can I keep working if the internet drops, or if I am offline?**
Partly. Nothing you have already saved is lost. Text you type into a passage keeps working: the message at the top right turns amber ("Offline — kept on this computer", or "Not saved yet — trying again" if your browser has not noticed yet), Setu keeps the text in your browser, and it sends the text by itself when the connection comes back. Leave the tab open until the message says "All changes saved". Answers, notes, pairing, unpairing and turning pages need the computer that runs Setu, so they fail while the connection is down. For all of these except notes, a red message appears at the bottom of the screen (in Chrome it reads "Failed to fetch", and other browsers word it differently). An answer that fails is not saved and its button does not light up, so press it again when you are back online. A note that fails says "The note could not be saved" beside the note box instead, and its words stay in the box. Click into the box and out again to send them.

**A page would not turn and a red message appeared, such as "Failed to fetch". Can I keep pressing ›?**
No. The screen stays on the old page, but Setu has already counted the turn, so the next press that works skips a page. The line under the book's title, such as "page 3 of 148", always names the page you are really looking at, even when the page box says something else. Once the connection is back, press ‹ once and wait for the page to change, then press › once. Typing a page number is not a reliable fix here, because typing the page that Setu has already counted does nothing.

**A message flashed at the bottom of the screen and vanished. What did it say?**
Messages there disappear on their own, after about three seconds for an ordinary one and about seven for a red one. If something did not work and you missed the message, do it again and read it. A failed answer also leaves its message beside the note box, and text that could not be sent shows in the message at the top right.

**I stopped yesterday. How do I get back to where I was?**
Use **Continue where you left off**, the first group in the left panel. Check that your name is in the "Your name" box at the top right, choose your books in its dropdown, read the small print under it, and press **Open it again**. Setu opens the same two books, puts each book on the page you were on, and brings back the view you were using. Section 3 of the manual has the steps. Setu does not remember which card you had selected, and it always starts in Read mode, so switch **Correct it** on again if you need it.

**"Continue where you left off" is missing, or it puts me on the wrong page.**
The group appears once any pair of books has been opened on this copy of Setu. Its dropdown lists the pairs of books where anybody has answered something, or where Setu remembers you being. Your place is kept under the name in the "Your name" box, so type it the same way each time (capital letters do not matter). With a different name, or none, Setu has no place for you: the small print has no "you were on page" part, and the books open on their first pages, or on the matched pages if somebody pressed **⇄ These two pages match**. If you leave the box empty, you share one place with everybody else who left it empty, and two people who type the same name share one too. If your books are not in the list, open them by hand with **Open side by side**, with the same book on the left. Your work is still there.

**How do I find the next pair nobody has answered?**
Press `n`. If a card is selected, Setu finds the next unanswered pair after that one. With nothing selected (press `Esc` first), it finds the first unanswered pair in the book. Either way it turns the books to the pages that hold the pair's passages and selects it, even when the two pages are far apart. Pairs that have a passage on one side only are included, and you will need to decide about them (see "Pairing passages" below). When none are left, a message says "Nothing left unanswered after this point in the book." The **Saved work** tab can show the same pairs under "Not checked yet", but its list starts with the pairs that were changed most recently, so its first card is not always the earliest.

## The two columns

**What is the "machine-read text"?**
The text in both columns was read off scanned pictures of the printed pages by a computer program. Setu's buttons call it the parser. A person did not type the text, so it contains mistakes: wrong letters, missing words, scrambled formulas. Checking it is your job. Which passage goes with which was also suggested by a program, and it is only a suggestion until you have looked at it.

**What do the coloured boxes and outlines mean?**
The colour of a card's left edge is the kind of passage (paragraph, equation and so on), and the key is the row of small coloured labels at the bottom of the left panel. Orange is the exception: an orange left edge and an orange tag such as "no Marathi paired" mark a pair that has a passage on this side only. A dashed outline means the pair has not been answered, and a solid one means it has (a card you answered a moment ago stays dashed until the page is drawn again). A grey card with a dotted outline and the tag "not in any pair" belongs to no pair. A tinted card with a ring is one you have selected. A card that flashes green and shows the word "saved" has had a correction saved a moment ago, and a blue "corrected" tag means that card's text has been changed by hand. The other colours are handed out as Setu goes, so read the word on the card's top line and do not learn the colours.

**The left side shows Chapter 3 and the right side shows Chapter 5. Is something broken?**
No. Two editions of a book rarely start their chapters on the same page, so each side has its own chapter and page controls. Untick **Turn both pages together**, choose the chapter you want on each side, find the pages that match, and press **⇄ These two pages match**. Section 5 of the manual walks through it. You do not need matching pages to pair two passages, though (see "Pairing passages" below).

**The page number does not match my printed book. Why?**
The number on screen is the page's place in the book's scanned file, counting from 1. If the file starts with a cover or a blank page, that counts as page 1, so the number printed on the paper can differ. Files you download count from 0, so page 1 on screen is page 0 in the file.

**Why do some passages have no partner?**
The matching program pairs passages by looking for things that read the same in every language, such as formulas and numbers like 1.2, and by keeping the two books in order. A passage with no such anchor, or one that one edition cut differently, can be left without a partner, and some passages exist in only one edition. In the one pair of books that was measured, 29 pairs in every hundred had a passage on one side only. Setu leaves these for a person and does not invent a partner. Look for the partner in the other book first. If you find it, pair the two passages. If the two editions have cut the text differently, record Structural mismatch with a note. If the other book has nothing there, record Missing or incomplete.

**A passage is empty and says "the parser found no text here". What do I do?**
Empty means the machine could not read anything there. It does not mean the page is blank. Choose Missing or incomplete, which covers "one side is empty". In Correct it mode the empty box can be typed into, but typing a whole passage in from the page is transcribing and not checking, so ask your supervisor before you do it.

**What is a "block", and what is "pair #7"?**
A block is Setu's word for one piece of text on a page: a heading, a paragraph, an equation, a caption. This guide mostly says passage or card. A pair is a passage on the left together with its partner on the right, and "#7" is its number in these two books, counting from 0. Both cards of a pair carry the same number. After the number, "↔ p108" gives the page of the partner in the other book, so you can see at once when the partner is on a different page. A card that says "not in any pair" belongs to no pair. The number is a position in the list of pairs and not a name, and it changes when pairs are added or taken away (see "Pairing passages" below).

## Pairing passages

**A card says "no Marathi paired". What does that mean?**
It means the pair has a passage on this side only: the matching program found no partner for it. The card has an orange left edge, and the orange tag names the language that is missing ("no English paired" on a Marathi card). Select the card, and the box for the other side says "No Marathi passage is paired with this one." and tells you what to do. If the partner is on another page, turn that book to it, click it, and press **⇄ Pair these two**. If the Marathi book really has nothing there, answer Missing or incomplete. Often the partner does exist and the program left it standing alone too. Look near where you expect it in the other book for a card whose orange tag names the first language, for example "no English paired". That card is the other half of the same passage.

**How do I pair two passages that are on different pages?**
The pages do not have to match, and you do not need **⇄ These two pages match**. First make sure **Read** is chosen in the left panel, so that clicking selects cards, and click the first passage. Second, turn the other book to the page that holds the other passage: type the page number into that book's page box and press Enter. If **Turn both pages together** is ticked, untick it first, or the first book moves too. The first card stays selected. Third, click the other passage. The panel under the columns shows both texts, an amber bar under them says "These two are not a pair yet.", and the answer buttons are hidden. Fourth, read both texts. If they do not say the same thing, click one of the two cards again or press `Esc`, and nothing has changed. If they do, press **⇄ Pair these two**, or press the `p` key. A message says "Paired. Now answer: do these two say the same thing?", the amber bar is replaced by a strip that says the two are paired, both passages stay selected, and the answer buttons appear. Answer as usual. Section 5 of the manual has the same steps with more detail.

**What is the difference between "⇄ These two pages match" and "⇄ Pair these two"?**
They do different jobs. **⇄ These two pages match** is about pages. It tells Setu that the two pages on screen hold the same material, so that from then on both books turn together, and it saves that link for everybody who works on these two books. It does not change which passage goes with which. **⇄ Pair these two** is about passages. It makes the one card you selected on the left and the one you selected on the right a pair, whatever pages they are on. You never need to match pages in order to pair passages.

**The amber bar says "These two are not a pair yet." What do I do?**
You selected one card in each column, and Setu checked whether the two belong to the same pair. They do not, and Setu does not guess, so the answer buttons stay hidden until you decide. If the two passages say the same thing, press **⇄ Pair these two** (or the `p` key). If you did not mean to select the second card, click it again to let it go, or press `Esc` to clear everything. The bar also tells you when pairing would take a passage away from its present partner. That other passage is not deleted. It stays in the book as a pair of its own, with its text and any correction, and answers already given to the pairs that change go back to Not checked yet, with the old answers kept in a record you cannot see on screen. Setu never pairs two passages that you did not choose.

**I turned the page and the passage I selected is still selected. Is that a mistake?**
No, it is deliberate. A passage that fits on one English page often runs onto the next page in the other language, and the two halves of a pair can sit on different pages. So a selected card stays selected, and its text stays in the panel under the columns, while you turn the pages. Click the card again to let it go, select another card, or press `Esc` to clear everything.

**The matching program paired the wrong passages. How do I unpair them?**
Select the pair by clicking one of its cards. The strip under the two texts reads "Paired: English page 5 ↔ Marathi page 5. If these are not translations of each other, unpair them." and has an **Unpair** button. Press it, and your browser asks you to confirm. Press OK. Each passage becomes a pair of its own, and its card says "no Marathi paired" or "no English paired". Find each passage's real partner and pair them as described above, or answer Missing or incomplete for a passage that really has no partner. Nothing is deleted. The text and any corrections stay with their passages, the answer goes back to Not checked yet (the old answer is kept in a record you cannot see on screen), and a note you wrote stays with the pair that keeps the left-hand passage. If you unpaired by mistake, select the two passages again, one in each column, and press **⇄ Pair these two**. The answer you had is not put back, but you can give it again.

**The numbers on the cards, such as "pair #26", have changed. Did I lose something?**
No. The number is the pair's place in the list of pairs for these two books, counting from 0. It is a position and not a name. Unpairing adds a pair. Pairing two passages that each stood alone takes one away. Other pairings can do either. Whenever a pair is added or taken away, by you or by somebody else, every pair after it moves up or down by one, and the total on the counter at the top right changes by one too. Nothing is lost. Somebody else's pairing shows up once your page is drawn again. Do not write a pair number down and trust it tomorrow. Write down the page and the first words of the passage instead.

**Can I split a passage in two, or join two passages into one?**
No. Setu can pair and unpair passages, and that is all. If the two editions have cut the text differently, for example one keeps two sentences together and the other has them in two passages, answer Structural mismatch and write a note that says where the rest is, by its first words or its page, because pair numbers move.

## Text that looks wrong

**My text is full of dollar signs, backslashes and angle brackets. Is it broken?**
No. A formula is written between dollar signs as code, for example `$A = \{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}$`, and a table is written as a run of tags, which are short codes in angle brackets, such as `<table><tr><td>`. That is how the parser stores them. Compare the numbers and symbols on the two sides, and read the words inside the tags.

**The words look wrong: letters swapped, a word missing, a formula scrambled. What do I do?**
That is what this work is for. Switch to **Correct it**, fix what is wrong, click outside the box, and choose Needs correction. You will often have no picture of the printed page, so correct only what you can be sure of from the two texts. If you cannot tell what the text should say, choose Unclear and put in a note what stopped you.

**The printed book itself has a spelling mistake. Do I fix it?**
No. Fix what the machine got wrong so that the text matches the printed page, and leave the book's own mistakes as they are, because the corpus should record what is printed. Put a note on your answer saying that the mistake is in the book. If you cannot tell whether an error is the book's or the machine's, choose Unclear. Setu does not enforce this rule, so if your supervisor gives you a different one, follow theirs.

**Where are the pictures, and what do I do about tables?**
Setu shows text only. A picture has no text, so it does not appear at all unless you tick **Include headers and page numbers**, when it shows as a grey "Figure" card that belongs to no pair and cannot be answered. A caption under a picture is text and appears as an ordinary pair. The project's wording puts picture captions under Not applicable, so ask your supervisor whether you should check them. A table appears as a run of tags with the cell contents in between, so compare the contents cell by cell, in order.

**I do not understand the passage, or I cannot read the script well. Do I have to answer it?**
Do not guess. Choose Unclear and write a note that says what you could not judge, so that a reviewer can pick it up. If a whole chapter or more is beyond you, stop and tell your supervisor.

## Choosing answers

**Needs correction or Structural mismatch?**
Ask whether the two passages are the same piece of the book. If they are, and the text has a mistake in it such as a wrong number, a typo or a garbled formula, choose Needs correction. If they are not the same piece, or one side has cut in two what the other keeps together, choose Structural mismatch and say in a note where the rest is.

**Missing or incomplete, or Not applicable?**
Ask whether something should be there. If text that belongs in the book is absent or empty on one side, choose Missing or incomplete. If nothing was meant to be there, because the passage is a page header, a decoration or a label that needs no translation, choose Not applicable.

**Some answer buttons are grey and will not work. Why?**
Exact, Needs correction and Structural mismatch compare two passages, so they only work on a pair that has a passage on both sides. When a card says "no Marathi paired" (or names whichever language is missing), only one passage is there, and those three buttons are greyed out. Point at one and a note says "There is only one passage here, so there is nothing to compare it with. Pair it first, or answer Missing or incomplete." If you press one anyway, or its number key (1, 2 or 4), Setu refuses and a red message says there is nothing to compare with. Missing or incomplete, Unclear and Not applicable work on every pair. If the partner is on another page, pair the two passages (see "Pairing passages") and the three buttons come back.

**I answered Exact on a passage that had nothing on the other side. What now?**
Today Setu stops this: on a card with a passage on one side only, Exact, Needs correction and Structural mismatch are greyed out, and pressing one gives a red message. An older version of Setu allowed it, so such an answer may still be in the saved work. Open the **Saved work** tab, choose "Needs attention" in the second box, and look for a card with an amber edge and a warning that starts "Answered as a pair, but there is nothing on the right." (or "on the left"). If the other book really has nothing for that passage, press **Change to “Missing or incomplete”** on the card. If you think the partner is somewhere else, press **Open in Annotate**, which takes you to Annotate with the book on the right page and the passage selected. Find the partner, click it, and press **⇄ Pair these two**. Pairing puts the answer back to Not checked yet, so that you can answer it properly.

**I pressed the wrong number. Can I undo it?**
Press the right number. The new answer replaces the old one, and the old one stays in a record that you cannot see on screen. Your note stays as it was, because each pair keeps its own note. To put the pair back to Not checked yet instead, open **Saved work**, find the pair and press **Undo my answer**.

**I think my answer went to a different pair from the one I meant.**
If you select a card on the left and a card on the right that are not the same pair, an amber bar appears and the answer buttons disappear, so an answer cannot land on a pair you did not mean. To check where an answer will go, read the line above the two texts, for example "pair #26". If an answer did go to the wrong pair, select that pair and press the right answer, or open **Saved work**, find the pair and press **Undo my answer** to put it back to Not checked yet. Then select the pair you meant and answer it.

**The note I typed has vanished.**
A note saves by itself, about a second after you stop typing and again when you click outside the note box, so you can write it before or after you press an answer. It stays in the box after you answer. Each pair has its own note, so if you selected a different pair, the box shows that pair's note. Select the first pair again to see yours. If a red message says the note could not be saved, the words are still in the box. Click into the box and out again to send them once the problem has passed.

**Do I have to answer every passage, and in order?**
Setu does not force you to do either. You can work in any order, and a pair you leave stays "Not checked yet", which tells the next person that nobody has looked at it. The aim of the work is that every pair gets an answer, so ask your supervisor which chapters are yours. The counter at the top right counts every pair in the two books, including passages that exist on one side only, so it cannot reach its total unless those get an answer too. The `n` key takes you to the next pair nobody has answered.

**What does the counter "12 / 21 answered" count?**
The first number is the pairs that have an answer, and the second is all the pairs in the two books you have open. It counts everybody's answers, and it includes passages that have no partner. It goes up by one each time a pair gets its first answer, and down when a pair goes back to Not checked yet, for example after **Undo my answer** or because its pairing changed. It updates when you answer, pair, unpair or undo. It does not change when somebody else answers until you do one of those.

## When the screen does something odd

**The answer buttons have disappeared.**
The buttons show when exactly one pair is selected: either one card (its partner appears in the other box), or one card on each side that belong to the same pair. They are hidden when nothing is selected, when several cards are selected (with Ctrl-click or Shift-click), when you have selected a header or page number that belongs to no pair, and when you have selected one card on each side that are not a pair. In that last case an amber bar under the two texts says "These two are not a pair yet." and offers **⇄ Pair these two**. Press `Esc` and click one card in the left column to start again. Turning the page does not clear your selection, so that is not the cause.

**I click on a card and nothing happens.**
There are two likely causes. The Tool switch in the left panel may be on Drag to crop, which happens when the `c` key is pressed by accident. Click "Click a block" under Tool, or press `c` again. Or **Correct it** may be switched on, which makes the text of each card a place to type: clicking the text puts your cursor in it and does not select the card. Click the small line at the top of the card, or click **Read** in the left panel.

**Passages have vanished from the column.**
Look at **Show only** at the bottom of the left panel. If it is not on "All kinds of block", it hides every other kind of passage from both columns the moment you choose it. A kind that is on one page and not on the other leaves the second column with nothing to show, and that column then says "The parser found nothing on page 4. Try the next page.", even though the page has passages. Set **Show only** back to "All kinds of block". Also check that **Dim the rest** is not ticked, because it fades every card you have not selected, and that **Include headers and page numbers** is the way you want it.

**I pressed some keys and things changed: the columns became editable, or clicks stopped working.**
The letters `e`, `c`, `n`, `p` and `?` and the number keys 1 to 6 are shortcuts. `e` switches Correct it on and off, `c` switches the Tool between Click a block and Drag to crop, `n` selects the next pair nobody has answered and turns the books to its pages, `p` pairs two selected cards when the amber bar is showing, `?` opens the list of shortcuts, and a number key answers the selected pair. Press `e` or `c` again to switch either one back. If a number key answered the wrong pair, see "I think my answer went to a different pair". The shortcuts switch off while your cursor is in a box, so click into a box before you type anything.

**Which keyboard shortcuts are there?**
The **?** button at the top right of the screen opens a list of them, and so does pressing the `?` key. `1` to `6` answer the selected pair. `←` and `→` turn the page: both books turn if **Turn both pages together** is ticked, and only the left book turns if it is not. `Alt` with an arrow turns the left book only, and `Shift` with an arrow turns the right book only, and both turn if the box is ticked. `n` selects the next pair nobody has answered. `p` pairs the two selected cards when the amber bar is showing. `e` switches between Read and Correct it. `c` switches between Click a block and Drag to crop. `Esc` clears the selection. `+` and `-` zoom the page pictures, and do nothing you can see in the Text view. Holding `Ctrl` hides the block labels in the Page + blocks view. All of these switch off while your cursor is in a box, so a 1 typed into a correction is a 1 and not an answer. Section 11 of the manual has the table, and a warning about `Alt` with `←` in some browsers.

## Correcting text

**How do I correct text, and how do I know it saved?**
Switch to **Correct it**, click into the text of a card, fix it, and stop typing. There is no save button. Setu saves about a second after you stop, and straight away when you click outside the box. The message at the top right says "Saving…" and then "All changes saved", the card flashes green for a second or two, and the word "saved" and a blue "corrected" label appear on its top line. The small line beside the note box may keep showing "…" after a correction, and you can ignore it, because the message at the top right is the one to trust. Then choose your answer. Section 7 of the manual has the full steps.

**I can type in the column and in the panel under it. Which one should I use?**
Either. Both show the same passage, and whatever you type in one appears in the other at once. The panel has one extra use: it can correct a partner that is on a page that is not on screen. To use it, select the pair first, by clicking the small line at the top of a card.

**I deleted everything in a passage so that I could retype it, and the old text came back. Why?**
An empty passage is never saved. While the box is empty nothing is sent, and the message at the top right may keep saying "Saving…". If you type the new text before you click away, it is saved like any other correction. If you click away while the box is still empty, the earlier text comes back and a red message says: "The passage was left empty, so nothing was saved and the text is back. To mark it as wrong, use an answer instead." If the passage should not be in the book at all, answer Not applicable. If the other book is missing it, answer Missing or incomplete.

**I pasted text into a passage. Will it bring fonts or colours with it?**
No. Pasting into a passage pastes plain text only, without fonts, colours or links, so text copied from a web page or a document arrives as plain words.

**The "corrected" label is on one card but not on its partner. Is something wrong?**
No. The label belongs to one passage and not to the pair. If the English text was corrected, only the English card has the label. If you see it on a passage you did not touch, somebody else corrected that passage. Clicking into a box and out again without changing anything does not add the label. The Saved work tab does the reverse: a pair shows "corrected" if either side was corrected, and the two texts under it say which one.

**How do I undo a correction?**
Select the card by clicking its top line. With **Correct it** switched on, a button called **Put back what the parser read** appears next to the label above the box, on each side whose text has been corrected, and only on that side. Press it to restore the machine's original text for that side. That restores the machine's original text, not your previous version, and your corrected version stays in the history.

## Working with other people

**Can two of us use the same link at the same time?**
Yes. Each of you sees your own screen, and answers, corrections and pairings go into one shared record as you make them. Your screen does not update when somebody else answers or pairs. You see their work the next time a page is drawn, for example when you turn the page. A pairing that somebody made shows "paired by hand" in the strip under the two texts. Setu has not been tested with a large group, so agree in advance who takes which chapter.

**What if we both answer the same pair?**
The later answer replaces the earlier one, and neither of you is told. The earlier answer stays in a record that you cannot see. Two people correcting the same passage is different: Setu asks the second person which version to keep, as the next question describes.

**What happens if two of us correct the same passage?**
Setu stops a save that would overwrite somebody else's newer correction. A box opens, headed "Someone else changed this passage while you were typing", with "Theirs — what is saved now" and "Yours — what you typed" side by side, and the message at the top right says "1 change needs you" until you choose. **Keep mine** saves your text as the newest version, and theirs stays in the history. **Keep theirs** leaves what is saved and throws away what you typed, although the box says that neither version has been thrown away. To keep your words, select them in the "Yours" box and copy them (Ctrl+C) before you press Keep theirs. If you are not sure which is right, copy yours, press Keep theirs, and ask whoever made the other change. The Saved work tab shows who last changed each pair.

**Does anyone see my name?**
Setu attaches the name you typed to every answer, note, correction and pairing, and uses it to remember where you were. The Saved work tab shows the name of the last person who changed each pair, so other annotators can see yours there. It also appears in the `updated_by` column of the Excel, CSV, TSV, JSON, JSON Lines and Parquet downloads, and whoever runs Setu can look it up. If you leave the box empty your work is still saved, but it carries no name, and your place is shared with everybody else who left the box empty. Setu has no accounts or passwords, so anyone who has the link can use it under any name. Treat the link like a key, and share it only with people who are annotating.

**Can I see or undo somebody else's answers?**
The **Saved work** tab lists everybody's answers, and each card shows the name of the last person who changed that pair. You can replace an answer by answering the pair again. **Undo my answer**, despite its name, removes the answer whoever gave it, so use it only after agreeing with whoever gave the answer first. If you think somebody else's answer is wrong and you cannot reach them, tell your supervisor.

## Saved work

**How do I find a pair I answered earlier?**
Open the **Saved work** tab. Choose your textbook in the first box and "Everything I have answered" in the second, or type words from the passage into the search box, which looks in both languages. Click the card, or press **Open in Annotate** on it, and Setu takes you to the Annotate tab with the books on that pair's pages and the pair selected. The top line of a card shows the pair's number, its answer, the pages of its two passages (for example p4 and p5) and the name of the last person who changed it. A "one side only" tag on a card means the pair has a passage on one side only. Only 25 cards show at a time, and **Older ›** shows the next 25. When a search or a filter finds nothing, the list says "Nothing has been answered yet — open two textbooks and start in the Annotate tab." That does not mean your work has gone. It means no pair matches.

**What does "Needs attention" mean?**
It is a choice in the second box of the Saved work tab. It lists the pairs that Setu thinks need another look, and shows how many there are, for example "Needs attention (3)". Each card has an amber edge and a warning at the top, and there are two kinds. "Answered as a pair, but there is nothing on the right." (or on the left) means somebody gave Exact, Needs correction or Structural mismatch to a pair that has only one passage. See the question above about answering Exact on a passage with nothing opposite it. "A side was saved empty." means the saved text of a passage is empty, which older versions of Setu allowed. Setu repairs most of these by itself every time it is started, and this list catches any that are left. Press **Open in Annotate**, switch on **Correct it**, and type the text or press **Put back what the parser read**. A pair leaves the list as soon as the problem is fixed.

## Downloading

**Which format should I download?**
Choose **Excel workbook**, the button with `.xlsx` on it, if you do not know which you need. It is a spreadsheet with one row for each pair, and it opens in Excel, LibreOffice or Google Sheets. In the Download tab each of the eleven formats is a button, and clicking one downloads the file at once, with the choices in the three boxes at the top applied. If someone tells you which format they need, choose that one.

**Some passages are missing from my download.**
Plain text, Moses, TMX and Hugging Face dataset can only hold pairs where both sides have text, so passages with no partner, or with an empty side, are left out of those four. Setu does not tell you on screen how many were left out. The other seven formats include every pair. The full bundle holds every format that can be produced on this copy of Setu, so its files of those four kinds leave the same passages out.

**How do I download only the pairs I have answered?**
Choose "Only what has been answered" in the second box at the top of the Download tab before you press a format button. It includes everybody's answers, not only yours.

**The Download tab says the bundle holds a "dataset card". Where is it?**
There is no separate card. The bundle is a zip with one folder for each format. The only description inside is a short README file in the Hugging Face dataset, which is a zip inside the zip.

## Practical questions

**How long does a book take?**
We do not know yet, because there is no measured pace for this work. The counter at the top right tells you how many pairs the two books hold, and the one pair of books that was measured held 2,304. Work for an hour, divide the pairs you answered by the hours, and you have your own pace. Ask your supervisor what they expect.

**Can I work on a phone or a tablet?**
Not properly on a phone. On a narrow screen the controls panel covers the columns, the button that hides it cannot be brought back without reloading the page, the panel under the columns overlaps the right column, and the page controls are inside the hidden panel. A tablet has not been tried, and the full layout needs a screen wider than 1,000 pixels. Use a laptop or desktop computer if you can.

**Can I use a dictionary or a translation website to help me?**
Nothing in Setu stops you, and nothing in it needs one. Whether your project allows it is your supervisor's decision, so ask before you rely on one.

**Who do I ask?**
Ask your supervisor about anything to do with the work: what to answer, what to correct, how much to do. Ask whoever gave you the link about anything that stops Setu working, such as a link that will not open. Tell them your name, the two books, the page on each side, the first words of the passage, the pair number as you saw it (it can change when pairs change), what you expected and what happened, and copy the words of any red message.
